Engineering case study — Android

Wayo

A native Android app that remembers exactly where you parked and turns your phone into a live compass pointing straight back to it.

Kotlin Jetpack Compose Material 3 Room Play Billing Solo build API 26–37

The problem it solves

Most parking-finder apps either run background GPS tracking the whole time you're driving — a battery and privacy cost most people don't want to pay — or hand you a flat map pin with no real sense of which way or how far. Wayo does neither: nothing runs until you deliberately tap "mark this spot," and from that moment on the app behaves like a directional locator, not a map — a rotating compass needle and a live distance readout, closer in spirit to Apple's Find My "Precision Finding" than to a maps app.

What it does

  • ＋Mark a spot — captures GPS coordinates, with an optional photo and a text note attached. Camera permission is fully optional; the app works without it.
  • ＋Live compass back to it — a custom-drawn dial (not a rotated stock icon) tracks bearing and distance in real time, fed by GPS plus the phone's orientation sensors.
  • ＋Arrival detection — the app tells you when you're actually within reach of the spot, factoring in how accurate the original GPS fix was, not just live signal quality.
  • ＋History, unlocked once — Pro users keep every past spot (photo, note, coordinates, timestamp); free users get the live locator only. One-time purchase — no subscription, no account required.
  • ＋Light and dark themes — a deliberate teal-on-navy palette (dark-first, since this is realistically opened in a dim parking garage), with the user's explicit toggle choice persisted independently of the system setting.
  • ＋Built for current Android — targets Android 17 (API 37) with edge-to-edge enforced, adaptive layout for large/foldable screens, and the modern Splash Screen API.

Architecture

Single-Activity, 100% Jetpack Compose, MVVM. A typed Navigation 3 back stack replaces any hand-rolled screen-state logic, and one reactive pipeline drives the entire navigation screen:

activeSpot (Room) → flatMapLatest → combine(GPS, compass) → NavigationState → Compose UI

Scoped deliberately: GPS and the compass sensor only subscribe while there's an active spot to navigate to, and stop the instant it's cleared — not after a delay.

ui/

Screens, shared components, and the full Material 3 theme.

data/

Room entity, DAO, and a thin repository — the only thing that touches persistence.

location/

Fused Location Provider wrapper with its own accuracy strategy.

sensor/

Rotation-vector compass with a device-fallback chain and smoothing.

billing/

Google Play Billing wrapper for the one-time unlock.

util/

Bearing/distance math and EXIF-aware photo handling.

Technical highlights

The parts worth talking through in an interview — real bugs that were found and root-caused, not just features that were added.

A GPS fix you can actually trust

Marking a spot doesn't grab whichever location arrives first. captureAccurateLocation() watches a short, timeout-bounded burst of live updates and keeps the best one seen — because a single fast-but-rough network-based fix silently poisons every future distance reading against that anchor point, even once live GPS is working perfectly.

An arrival radius that's honest about uncertainty

"You've arrived" combines two independent error sources — live GPS accuracy and the accuracy of the original capture — via quadrature (root-sum-square), the statistically correct way to combine them, then clamps the result to an 8–20m band so it's never impossibly strict or uselessly loose.

Smoothing without drift

The live distance readout runs through a symmetric low-pass filter to kill GPS jitter. An earlier asymmetric version (fast to react to increases, slow to decrease) was deliberately rejected — it acts as a one-way ratchet under noise and drifts the displayed distance upward over time even while standing still.

A compass that degrades gracefully

Tries the fused rotation-vector sensor first, falls back to raw accelerometer + magnetometer, and falls back again to GPS course-over-ground on devices with no usable orientation sensor at all — confirmed to genuinely happen on some hardware despite the spec sheet. Axis remapping accounts for the device's current display rotation.

Billing that survives refunds and new devices

Pro status is cached locally for an instant "unlocked" UI on launch, but reconciled against a real Play Billing query every time — so a refund, chargeback, or a purchase restored on a new device is always reflected correctly, not just trusted forever from a local flag.

Theming taken all the way through

A full custom Material 3 color scheme — including the tonal/container roles most apps leave on Material's default purple — matched end-to-end to the splash screen, launcher icon, and native window background, so there's no flash of a mismatched color before Compose paints its first frame.

Ready-to-use talking points

  • Designed and built Wayo, a native Android parking-locator app, end to end — architecture, UI, sensor integration, and Play Store monetization — as a solo project in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose.
  • Implemented a sensor-fusion navigation pipeline combining GPS and device orientation sensors, including a statistically-grounded uncertainty model (quadrature error combination) to decide arrival honestly rather than by a fixed distance.
  • Diagnosed and fixed a subtle asymmetric-smoothing bug that caused displayed distance to silently drift upward over time — replaced with a symmetric low-pass filter after reasoning through the failure mode.
  • Integrated Google Play Billing for a one-time purchase, including purchase acknowledgment, cross-device restore reconciliation, and cache-first state for instant UI response.
  • Built a complete custom Material 3 theming system — light and dark palettes, full tonal/container role coverage — matched end-to-end across splash screen, launcher icon, and in-app UI.
